Which road was the car really on?—

GPS points drift off the road: onto the next street, across buildings, or into gaps. For every GPS point we pick the road segment the vehicle was really on. A machine-learning classifier scores the nearby roads, and an HMM with Viterbi decoding joins those scores into one connected route.

Why metres?

At this latitude, one degree of longitude is about 75 km but one degree of latitude is about 111 km. Over a few kilometres, a local flat projection gives accurate metres.

How are labels made?

The route lists arcs in driving order, not one per point. Each point's label is the nearest route arc within 30 m, preferring one that points the same way as the car. Points the route doesn't cover are left out.

What does the classifier see?

One row per (GPS point, candidate road) pair, answering: is this the true road, yes or no? For each GPS point, the road with the highest score is the model's pick.

Sequence decoder

Hidden Markov Model + Viterbi

The classifier judges each GPS point on its own, so it can jump between roads that don't connect. The HMM looks at the whole trip and picks the most likely chain of roads.

score(roadt) = max over roadt−1 [ score(roadt−1) + transition(roadt−1 → roadt) ] + log P(roadt | GPSt)

Pieces

  • Hidden state: the candidate road of each GPS point
  • Emission: log of the classifier probability
  • Transition: 0 for the same or next connected arc, −1 per extra arc (up to 3), a big penalty if not connected

Edge cases

  • GPS gap > 10 s: any road change is allowed
  • Unconnected roads: penalised, not forbidden, so the decoder never gets stuck
  • Penalties: tuned on 15 held-out training trips

Dataset · from results/eda and results/features

What the data looks like

Each trip has a GPS track (lon, lat, time, about 1 per second), road nodes, road arcs (from node → to node) and a reference route (an ordered list of arc ids).

Key finding: the reference route doesn't always cover the whole track

In some training trips the route covers only part of the GPS track. In trip 54 only about 12% of points are within 30 m of the route. We label and score only the points that have a reference road nearby, and report this coverage openly.
